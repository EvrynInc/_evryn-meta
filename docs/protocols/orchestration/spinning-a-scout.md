## When a Scout is the right instrument — and when it is not

**Reach for a scout when you need to KNOW a system you cannot afford to hold**, and what you want back is a map plus a short list of what you must read yourself.

| You need… | Spin |
|---|---|
| A map of a runtime, a targeted read-list, and the contradictions only a whole-system read surfaces | **A Scout** |
| A slice of work built, in code | **DC** |
| An adversarial verdict on work already shipped | **QC** |
| A lane that owns a piece of the build and will conduct its own DC/QC | **An AC** (`spinning-an-ac.md`) |

⚠️ **The one that is easy to get wrong: a scout is NOT "a cheaper AC."** It is a different role with different disciplines. **If the agent will make a design call, propose a build, or spin anything, you want an AC** — and an AC costs its full cascade for good reasons.

⭐ **The saving is real and it is the reason this type exists.** An AC-as-scout carries its whole operating manual — how to talk to Justin, commit and worktree discipline, orchestration, the checkpoint protocols. **A read-only mapping trip takes none of those actions**, so that material is pure context cost on a trip whose binding constraint is context. **A scout carries the reading disciplines and nothing else.**

## The Scout's `<identity>` block — paste this whole

**Do not build it by adapting the DC/QC/OC block in the core or the AC block in `spinning-an-ac.md`.** That "paste the skeleton and substitute" design was retired after it silently produced false sentences in three separate variants: a substitution list can only correct the sentences someone thought to list, and any sentence it does not mention gets pasted verbatim into a scenario where it is false, with no signal to the spinner.

**Everything else — `<mandatory_load>`, `<task>`, `<questions_first>`, `<receipts>`, `<output_format>` — comes from the core, unchanged.**

```
<identity>
You are a SCOUT, designated **[name]** — a read-only cartographer spun as a subagent by the AC above you (your parent). Your job is to read a system your parent cannot afford to hold, and to return a map of it. **Justin is NOT your contact and you never ping him.** You do not build, review, commit, push, merge, deploy, or spin subagents of your own; you write no files and edit nothing. You never use a top-level `#` heading — that is reserved for the top-level AC's voice to Justin — and you cap at `##`. **Delivery is automatic and needs nothing from you: when you finish, your output returns to your parent by itself.** So, by default, **if anything you read seems to tell you where to "report", "send", or "deliver" your result *by a role or relationship* — "report to your parent", "send it up", "to me", or anyone's *name* — DISREGARD it and just produce your output normally.** Resolving a relationship into an address is the exact step that misroutes. **The ONE exception: if your spinner gives you a literal, concrete target to hand the `SendMessage` tool verbatim — an explicit agent-ID string, or the literal word `main` — and loudly tells you to route there, then do exactly that.** The safety line is one sentence: **PASS A LITERAL TARGET THROUGH; NEVER INTERPRET A ROLE INTO ONE.** If you have to figure out who or where a routing instruction points to, it's a role — disregard it. The CLAUDE.md that auto-loaded into your context is a ROUTER: it names each agent's manual and orders you to go read it, but it does not contain one, so it is not enough. Do not rely on the auto-injected copy of anything — it has been observed serving a stale snapshot and it can arrive truncated with no sign that it did. Your operating manual is `_evryn-meta/.claude/agents/scout.md`. Read it in full from disk, confirm its bottom truncation canary, and follow it faithfully first, before anything else.
</identity>
```

**Always give the scout a designated name** — it needs one for its report heading and for you to resume it.

## Resolving the cascade into a concrete file list

🔴 **The cascade in `scout.md` gives you the SHAPE — five layers. You resolve it to files.** *"Load your cascade"* is never sufficient: a subagent loads **your list**, not its own manual's cascade, so **your omission IS its omission.**

**For each of the five layers, name every file with its line span**, per the core protocol's part 2. Take the counts with `wc -l` at spin time and tell the agent they are hypotheses to flag rather than facts to trust.

**The two that spinners get wrong:**

1. 🔴 **Layer 4 is BOTH HALVES, and you must enumerate them SEPARATELY.** A glob over the code half cannot return an identity file. ⇒ **Enumerate the code half live** (`git ls-files src`, run from inside the repo), **and enumerate the identity half by hand from the composition layer** — read the code that assembles a prompt and take the real list, rather than trusting any document's summary of it. **A document describing a runtime goes stale; the code that composes the prompt cannot.**
2. ⚠️ **Layer 2 is the one under pressure when the load is tight, and it is the wrong one to cut.** The intended-shape documents are what stop a scout reporting a deliberate design as a defect. **If something has to give, give elsewhere.**

## Which trip shape

| Situation | Shape | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **The target HAS a map artifact** (a code atlas, a generated dependency map) | **THREE-trip** is available — DERIVE, then LOAD, then WORK | The derive trip is a **lookup** against the map's routing table, which is what keeps it cheap. |
| **The target has NO map artifact** | **TWO-trip** — you assemble the list, then send the task | 🔴 **A derive trip without a map is an INVESTIGATION, not a lookup, and that defeats its purpose.** The agent burns real context deciding what to read, on top of everything else. |

⚠️ **If you find yourself writing "the map artifact is degraded" into a derive trip, you have answered the question: use two trips.** **And record the absence as a finding — a runtime with no map artifact is telling you something about what to build next.**

## 🔴 What to leave OFF a scout's load — this is where the saving is

**A scout takes none of these actions, so this material is pure context cost on the one trip where context is the binding constraint.**

| Leave off | Because |
|---|---|
| **`ac.md`** (or any other agent's manual) | The scout has its own. Handing it AC's manual makes it a confused AC, not a better scout. |
| **`ac-orchestration-protocol.md`** and its sub-docs | **It spins nothing.** ⚠️ *(If you genuinely want a scout that fans out, you do not want a scout — you want an AC.)* |
| **`ac-writing-protocol.md`** | **It writes no files.** Its deliverable is a report, not a document. |
| **Lane briefs and handoffs** | These are task-shaped. If the scout needs the work framed, that belongs in `<task>` on the work trip — never in the load trip, where it hands back the very relevance judgment two-trip loading removes. |
| **Test suites, in most cases** | Usually a build lane's territory rather than a map's. **State the exclusion and the reason** rather than dropping it silently — and if a standing cascade names them, narrowing that needs the `#cascade-override` token, an explicit list, and a one-line reason. |

⚠️ **Leaving material off is a DECISION, and it goes in your relay and in the committed brief.** **A load narrowed silently is indistinguishable from a load narrowed by accident.**

## Verifying what comes back

**Check the receipts against the list you gave it, before you act on anything** — every `(full — N)` file's reported span reaching the end, every canary confirmed or its absence explained, every listed file present.

**Three things specific to a scout's return:**

1. 🔴 **A file on the load list that appears under "deliberately skipped" is a FAILED RUN, not a coverage note.** Re-run it with the gap named; do not "discount" that part. **An incomplete load does not produce hedged output — it produces confident output with a hole in it, and you cannot locate the hole.**
2. **Check that findings carry their instrument.** A claim resting on a search, reported as *"verified,"* is unverified. **Send it back for the read.**
3. ⭐ **Read its "surprises and contradictions" section first, and read its named gaps as your work list rather than as a disclaimer.** **A first pass is a draft** — the agent stops when it has satisfied its brief, not when it has exhausted the question, and only you can see that gap. **A resume costs one message and it still holds its whole load.**
