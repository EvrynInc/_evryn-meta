## The vocabulary

| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| **Admiral** | The top-level agent of a command structure **reporting directly to Justin** in a given piece of work. Writes the Captains' briefs, aggregates their output, and is **Justin's sole conduit** — he does not (typically) talk to Captains. **Owns the `#` heading**, because `#` marks the voice reporting to Justin (`ac-orchestration-protocol.md` → *Relaying to Justin*). *(Justin, 2026-09-10: "Admirals are — at least for now — by definition those who are reporting to me." ⏳ **Autonomy may move this definition; whoever switches autonomy on re-decides it here.**)* |
| **Captain** | An agent **commanding its own lane and reporting to an Admiral**, not to Justin. May spin its own subagents; that does not make it an Admiral. **Caps its headings at `##`, even in its own session** — Justin may be able to see that session, but it is not the voice reporting to him. |
| **The log** | The Captain's brief file, which both parties append to. **The channel of record.** |
| **A signal** | A direct session-to-session message (`SendMessage`). **Not the record.** |

**How these map onto the words you already use — and where not to use them.** *(Justin, 2026-09-10: "Not every spinner is an admiral — some are captains, some are subs themselves.")* A head AC is often an Admiral; a lane AC is almost always a Captain; *conductor* is close to a synonym for Admiral; a sub-AC is likely always a Captain. **Parent, spinner and sub stay as they are** — they name the spawning relationship, which covers many cases this pair does not. ⇒ **Use Admiral and Captain only where the reporting line to Justin is the point.**

⚠️ **The role grants a reporting line, not authority.** A top-level AC's merge authority comes from Justin's standing grant of 2026-06-02 (`ac-orchestration-protocol.md` → *The merge / ship rubric*), not from being an Admiral — **a founding-team Admiral has none.**

**The log-and-signal machinery below is for a STANDALONE Captain** — one in its own session, spun by Justin pasting a one-line command, which you cannot hold as a subagent. **A Captain you do hold as a subagent** returns its output to you by itself (`ac-orchestration-protocol.md` → *Nested subagents*) and runs on the work brief in `spinning-an-ac.md`.

## The standalone Captain's spin command — and why its ORDER is load-bearing

**Justin types it; you write the brief it points at.** Its shape, in his words:

> *"Load <name> — full cascade. **Only once you're fully loaded**, read and faithfully execute <brief>. **Do not read the brief before you are fully loaded.**"*

🔑 **That ordering IS two-trip loading, for a session you cannot resume** (`ac-orchestration-protocol.md` → *Load first, task second*). The cascade loads before the Captain can see its task, so it cannot score its load against the work and quietly drop whatever looks irrelevant. ⇒ **Keep the three sentences in that order, and keep the task out of the command itself.** Collapse it to one line — *"load and execute <brief>"* — and the guarantee is gone, with nothing to show that it went. What else the brief must carry is in the lobby's *Subagent or standalone instance* (*"the brief must need NOTHING from him"*).

## Justin's two standing blocks — verbatim

### Captains command their own ships

> *"I want to **make sure** we're treating Lane ACs as the captains of their ships. You're the admiral, but they're the one on the ground, and they need to be in command. They need to be properly loaded to actually **genuinely understand** their domain — in a way that you **can't** really — you have to stay at conductor altitude. I think the protocols say this, but it bears repeating: tell them their job, but make sure it's 100% clear to them that they have to bring their independent judgment — and if you've incorrectly scoped or made an incorrect assumption, they need to bounce it back to you and get clarity before they run forward."*

**The machinery for it already exists — use it rather than restating it:** a brief is a LEAD, and when the lane's read contradicts it, the two of you settle it before the lane proceeds (`spinning-an-ac.md` → *Lane-AC lifecycle*, "the authority runs both ways"); and say in the brief how much of the material you have actually read (`ac-orchestration-protocol.md` → *Tell the subagent how much of the system you have actually read*).

### The Admiral is Justin's sole conduit

> *"Assume these things about me: 1) I haven't read anything you're reading — including your brief. I have a good top-level (CEO) understanding of most of this, and I've read most of the source-of-truth docs within the past six months, but for the purposes of our work today, the best way for you to conceptualize things is: you are my **sole conduit to understanding**. Make sure your responses can stand on their own — don't write as though I've been reading over your shoulder — I definitely **have not**. 2) Even when it seems like I'm **right here**, I'm not — I'm coming in cold from other (often rather intense) meetings — sometimes after an hour or more. So give me little handholds. And make sure to ping me every time you have anything for me, for the duration of this session."*

**Why this block belongs to the Admiral rather than to general communication guidance:** the failure is *caused by* being deep in several lanes' context at once. **That is the Admiral's occupational hazard, not everyone's.**

**Every message to Justin must be a NUMBERED REPORT — "Report #1," "Report #2," and on.** *(Justin, 2026-09-10: "You're going to have reports piling up while I'm gone… title each report 'Report #1', etc. — so I can respond to each." And: "I'm not reading all of the little dribs and drabs you drop in along the way. Anything you need me to read needs to be in a numbered report.")* ⇒ **Status narration between tool calls is not a channel to him** — if he needs it, it goes in the next report. **Ballot numbers stay a separate, stable sequence inside the reports**, so an answer he gives days later still points at the right question. **Why this belongs to the Admiral:** heartbeats and Captains wake you while Justin is elsewhere, so your output piles up unread — the report number is how he finds what is his.

## The log and the signal

**A signal may not carry a decision, a finding, or anything that changes the work.** It may point (*"Entry 3 is up"*), nudge, or ask for something trivial. **Everything of substance goes in the log first, and the signal points at it.**

**The mechanism, because the rule looks like tidiness and is not:** a future instance of either party loads the log and nothing else. **Substance that lived only in signals is invisible to it — and worse than invisible, because the log reads as complete, so it never knows to look.** *(Justin: "they'll read the briefs and have an eerie feeling they're missing big chunks of the conversation — or worse, they *won't*.")*

**The tell:** more than about two lines into a signal means you are writing a log entry in the wrong place.

**And a signal is for YOUR OWN LANE — an Admiral and its Captains.** A session outside it gets reached through its own designated channel: an AC's mailbox (`_evryn-meta/docs/mailboxes/inbox-<name>.md`), or Justin if it is time-critical. **Some live sessions are dormant on Justin's instruction and ask not to be signaled — and `ListAgents` cannot tell you which: the roster tells you a session EXISTS, not whether it is available to you.** 

**Briefs and the logs inside them commit freely, in both repos — and nothing else does.** `_evryn-meta` pre-authorizes brief and mailbox traffic (`spinning-an-ac.md` → *The work brief*); `evryn-team-workspace` does the same for working briefs and their logs *(Justin, 2026-09-10: "You can all commit briefs as you go. I'd recommend it. I think of those in the bucket with mailbox files.")*. **A Captain's drafts are not briefs, even when a brief describes them** — they wait for Justin's vet, like memory files, project docs and anything source-of-truth. They can sit uncommitted safely: a file on disk survives a session restart. **Stage by explicit path and put the pathspec on the commit itself** — a tree mid-round holds several lanes' uncommitted work at once.

## A session address does not survive a restart. A file path does.

**What was measured on 2026-09-10:**
- **A signal is a genuine interrupt** — it wakes the recipient mid-turn. Nobody polls to receive one.
- 🔴 **After a restart, every local session came back under a new name AND a new ref, and nothing announced it.** A signal sent to the old address does not obviously fail.
- ⚠️ **The ref in brackets belongs to the running PROCESS, not the name and not the conversation.** It holds while a session runs, **even through a rename** — a session went from `jbm-xps-15-9510-lively-quilt` to `Act-18 handoff load list` when it acquired a title, and kept `a2219f` — **and it changes when the process restarts.** *(The 2026-09-10 restart was Justin updating VS Code and the Claude Code extension, which restarted every session at once.)* **The conversation itself survives a restart, but nothing found so far can address a message to it** — which is why the log stays the only address that survives. **Within one session's life, trust the ref over the name.**

As of 2026-09-10, this is a brand-new feature, so watch as it evolves — it may begin to work more reliably over time.

⇒ **Three habits, and they are the whole of it:**
1. **Publish your address on every start** — `ListAgents`, first line — in one known place: **the Admiral in its own brief, each Captain in its log's header.** Re-publish after any restart. **Never cache a peer's address anywhere else.**
2. **Before every signal, read the peer's published address and confirm it in `ListAgents`.** If it does not resolve, write to the log and let the watcher carry it.
3. **Arm a watcher on every log you are party to** — a persistent `Monitor` that fires when the file changes. **The watcher is what makes the log the reliable channel: it is an address that survives a restart, which a signal can never be.** Re-arm after any restart; background tasks die with their session.
   ```
   f="<path to the log>"; p=$(md5sum "$f" | cut -c1-32); while true; do sleep 30; c=$(md5sum "$f" 2>/dev/null | cut -c1-32); if [ "$c" != "$p" ]; then echo "log changed: $f"; p=$c; fi; done
   ```
   It fires on your own writes too; ignore those.

## Your own brief — current continuously, not at set-down

**An Admiral keeps a brief for ITSELF**, not just one per lane: the round's state, the Captains and their published addresses, Justin's rulings, the aggregation ledger, and what is open for him. **The discipline is the work brief's (`spinning-an-ac.md` → *The work brief*), turned on yourself** — and it earns a line here because the Admiral is the one agent whose death strands every lane at once.

🔑 **The test, at every append: *if this instance died right now, could a fresh one pick up from this file alone?*** If not, the brief is behind — fix it before doing anything else. Periodically, do a top-to-bottom read of the brief — you might have updated one part and created a contradiction with another part. 

**Record Justin's rulings **precisely** wherever the wording carries the meaning.** You are his sole conduit, so a ruling that lives only in your transcript dies with your session — and a paraphrase is what the next Admiral would inherit as his words.

## The aggregation duty

**Every source-of-truth change any Captain drafts lands in ONE list you hand Justin, naming who changed what and why.** He usually does not talk to Captains, so a change that is not on your list is one he reviews blind — or never learns to review.

- **Fill the ledger as Captains report, not at the end.** A ledger assembled at set-down is the one most likely to miss a lane that died mid-round.
- **Record a baseline commit per repo when the work starts**, so Justin can review the round's changes *in total* — `git diff <baseline> HEAD -- <path>` — rather than only the last edit. ⚠️ **A baseline goes stale when the repo moves under the round from elsewhere:** on 2026-09-10 one repo took nine commits from Justin's laptop mid-round, so its recorded baseline would have shown his own work as the round's. ⇒ **Re-check every baseline against `origin` before you hand over the ledger, and tell Justin which baseline he is looking at.**
- **Questions travel a different road.** A Captain's `NEEDS-JUSTIN` question goes up the escalation ladder in `spinning-an-ac.md` — surfaced or propose-absorbed, never silently dropped. **The ledger carries drafted changes awaiting his review; the ladder carries decisions awaiting his call.**

## Packing out — watch your own tokens, and re-anchor by asking

**The Admiral offers the packout itself, before the edge.** Justin, 2026-09-10:

> *"He pays attention to his tokens, and when he thinks (or measures, if that's available) that he has about 150k left, he pauses and asks me if I want to do a #packout."*

⚠️ **The tension, held honestly: an agent has no reliable way to read its own remaining context** (`spinning-an-ac.md` → *The work brief*). **Justin's resolution is to re-anchor, not to measure:**

> *"When you're getting close on tokens, I'll often give you a count and then you'll start keeping track and you're **pretty decent** at keeping track. So I'd say 'pay attention to how many tokens you think you've used, but ask Justin where you're at, so you can re-anchor.' I want you watching for yourself — and I don't want a bunch of static in terms of **manufactured** check-ins. Obviously, natural check-ins should occur, but I'd love for one part of your brain to be **at least occasionally** thinking about your token count."*

⇒ **Keep a running estimate; at a natural break, ask him for the true count; when you believe about 150k remain, pause and offer `#packout`.** **The always-current brief is what makes that moment cheap** — and the stakes are highest for you, because a compacted agent is done for the session (ADR-058) and every lane is standing on your brief.

- **Run `#packout` in full** (`docs/protocols/packout-protocol.md`).
- **A standalone Captain outlives your session; a subagent Captain dies with it** (`ac-orchestration-protocol.md` → *Subagent or standalone instance*). ⇒ **Before you pack out, every Captain's drafted changes must already be on the ledger.** Your successor finds each Captain through its log — re-publishing its own address, reading each log header for the Captain's current one, and re-arming the watchers.
- **A Captain packs out into its own log, and you verify it** — the log is its handoff, so the test above applies to it too.

### Closing a Captain — you take it through its own `#lock`

> *"I don't want to interact with them directly again until I'm closing their session — I want you to take them through their #lock at the end, and everything."* *(Justin, 2026-09-10.)*

**This is the step that makes "Justin does not talk to Captains" hold all the way to the end.** The close runs: **the Captain runs its own `#lock` and records it in its log → you verify it against that lock protocol → Justin closes the session.** Its commits follow the commit rule under *The log and the signal*.

### Two things that bite at a Captain's commits and at its compaction

**1. A go-ahead you relay is not Justin's go-ahead in the Captain's session.** The harness's permission check looks at who authorized an action *in the session taking it*, so *"Justin says commit"* arriving from you reads as a peer's instruction, not the user's — and it refuses. **Observed twice on 2026-09-10:** once on a Captain's commit that touched `CLAUDE.md`, and once on a Captain's commit of its own log, a file the brief-commit rule allows. ⇒ **Plan for it rather than discover it: when a Captain's commit needs Justin's go, ask him to type it in that Captain's session, or to tell you directly — then you make the commit from your own session.** ⚠️ **And never hand a refused action to another session to do instead** — a peer acting on a go its own user never gave is the same failure, moved. *(Justin, Ballot 34, 2026-09-10.)*

**2. When a Captain compacts with a subagent's report still unread, copy the report — don't recall it.** The report exists, complete, in the subagent's transcript on disk; after compaction it exists nowhere reliable in the Captain's context. ⇒ **Have the compacted Captain do one mechanical task and stop:** extract the report **verbatim** from the on-disk transcript **by script**, hash-verify the copy, and write it to a file headed *"Verbatim and unedited. UNVERIFIED: the parent instance had compacted before reviewing it."* A mechanical copy doesn't depend on compacted memory; a recalled summary does. **This is Justin's one sanctioned exception to "if you compact, you stop"** — it is a file-to-file copy, not judgment. The fresh Captain then *reviews* that file instead of re-running the subagent. *(Justin's idea, 2026-09-10; used on Captain Nathan's fact-audit re-check.)*
