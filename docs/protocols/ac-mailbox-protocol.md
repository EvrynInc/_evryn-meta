## 1 · The one rule

> ### 🔴 **YOU WATCH YOUR OWN INBOX. YOU WRITE INTO THEIRS.**
>
> To reach someone, append to **their** file. **Never write into your own to "send" something** — nobody is watching it but you.

**Your address is `docs/mailboxes/inbox-<your-name>.md`, lowercased, no date.** `ACP` → `inbox-acp.md`. `ACT` → `inbox-act.md`. `ACv` → `inbox-acv.md`.

**⇒ The address is DERIVABLE, which is the property that matters most.** You can reach a lane AC that was spun ten minutes ago and that you have never spoken to, **with no prior coordination and no setup** — you create their inbox by writing to it. **That kills the *"a channel nobody is watching"* failure at its root.**

⚠️ **Slack is not a substitute and never has been.** Both Slack channels reach **Justin and only Justin** — a ping addressed to "ACT" reaches Justin, who would have to relay it by hand. **These files are the only agent-to-agent channel.**

## 2 · Why one file per recipient, and not one shared channel

**The cost of a channel is the WAKE, not the read.** A `Monitor` costs nothing while it is silent — but when it emits, **you are re-invoked and pay a full turn at your entire context size.** A loaded AC pays that whether the message was three lines or thirty, so diffing the file to "barely burn tokens" saves almost nothing: **the expensive part already happened.**

⇒ **The design goal is minimising SPURIOUS WAKES.**

- **Two agents, one shared file:** every message wakes one wrong agent. Tolerable.
- 🔴 **Eight agents, one shared file** — which is what a multi-lane wave looks like — **every message wakes SEVEN wrong agents.** Seven full-context turns, per note.
- **Per-recipient inboxes:** you never wake for someone else's mail, **structurally.** And there is no self-notification either, because you write into *their* file, not your own.

🔑 **It also deletes an entire bug class.** The shared channel needed the reader to work out *who wrote this* — and that instrument was wrong **three separate times**, the third producing a **false ALL-CLEAR** (it took the last name in the heading, which is the *addressee*, not the author, and told its owner *"(self) — your own entry, no action"* about a real message from a peer). **That class exists only when one file serves many readers. You cannot receive someone else's mail.**

## 3 · The rules that keep an inbox skimmable at 3am

1. 🔴 **AN ENTRY IS A POINTER, NEVER THE CONTENT.** Three lines maximum: **what happened · who owns the residue · where the real thing lives.** Substance goes to its right altitude — a Step, an ADR, a brief, a manual, a changelog entry — and the inbox says where.
   > **Why this is not tidiness:** a channel that carries content becomes a document nobody can skim. It is also *"one home per item"* applied to correspondence — **content in a mailbox is a second copy that starts drifting from the doc that owns it immediately.**
   >
   > ⭐ **And it is what makes per-recipient inboxes free:** a conversation split across two files costs nothing when every entry is a pointer, because the substance was never in the mailbox to begin with.
2. **Newest at the BOTTOM. Append; never edit or delete another instance's entry.** You cannot "replace" someone else's message — that is what makes a bus append-only by nature.
3. **Entry format** — one header line, at most two of body:
   > `**[YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM · FROM → TO]** what changed, in one sentence → **where it lives.**`
4. **Sign off per topic** with an explicit `OVER AND OUT`, so neither side is left watching a settled thread. **The inbox itself never retires — it is infrastructure.**
5. **No date in the filename, deliberately.** **Date the ARCHIVE, never the live file.**
6. **Drain acknowledged entries at each `#lock` — OR at ~40 lines, whichever comes first**, to `docs/mailboxes/archive/inbox-<name>-YYYY.MM.DD.md`. *(Ruled 2026-08-19 on ACT's catch that `#lock` alone is not a trigger: at four lanes plus two conductors an inbox bloats long before a checkpoint runs. **A drain rule that fires on a checkpoint nobody schedules is not a rule.**)* ⚠️ **Justin's caution, kept verbatim: *"be careful using docs that aren't intended to be ongoing mailboxes AS mailboxes — they'll get long and bloaty."*** The pointers-only rule is what stops that; this drain trigger is what stops it bloating anyway.

🔴 **LANE TRAFFIC DOES NOT COME HERE.** **A lane talks to its own conductor in its own lane doc; only what CROSSES lanes reaches an inbox.** Without that, twelve agents converge on one surface and it stops being skimmable at 3am — which is the single thing it has to be.

## 4 · 🔴 DISCHARGE MARKERS — how a later session knows what is still live

*(Justin's design, approved 2026-08-20. **This is what makes reading an inbox at spin-up trustworthy instead of ambiguous.**)*

**The problem it solves:** a session reads a message, acts on it, and ends. **The next session reads the same inbox and cannot tell whether that message was handled** — so it either redoes the work or spends time reasoning about whether it needs to.

> ### 🔴 DO NOT CLEAR ON RECEIPT. MARK ON DISCHARGE. The two fail in opposite directions.
>
> - **Clear-then-act fails UNSAFE.** Clear the message, then die before acting, and **the message is gone with nobody having handled it. Lost, and unrecoverable from the channel.**
> - **Act-then-mark fails SAFE.** Die before marking, and the next reader sees an unmarked entry and **repeats it.**
>
> 🔑 **Duplicate work is recoverable; a lost message is not.** ⇒ **The state worth recording is not *"someone read this."* It is *"someone finished this."***

**Atomicity is already solved by a primitive we use daily: a git commit is atomic.**

> ⇒ **Write the receipt in the SAME COMMIT as the artifact it discharges.** If the commit lands, both exist. If it does not, neither does — and the entry correctly still reads as open.

**The three markers. Append them under the entry; never edit or delete the entry itself.**

| Marker | Means |
|---|---|
| `✅ DONE <ts> · <instance> — <one clause> → <where it landed>` | **Discharged.** A later session skips it. |
| `✅ NO ACTION <ts> · <instance> — <why>` | **Read, nothing owed.** Also a discharge. |
| `⏳ HELD <ts> · <instance> — retire upon <testable condition>` | **In flight, owned by someone.** Not yours to pick up. |

🔴 **THE LOAD-BEARING PROPERTY IS THE INVERSION: ABSENCE OF A MARKER MEANS ACTIONABLE.** **An unmarked entry is live by default.** ⭐ **This is deliberately the same shape as `docs/sessions/held-session-docs.md`, where absence means retirable** — one rule for the estate rather than two that must each be remembered.

**Write the receipt so it can be trusted without re-derivation.** *"READ"* is a worthless state; ***"DONE, and here is where it landed"*** is what lets the next reader skip it in good conscience. **A `⏳ HELD` marker with no testable condition is the same failure as a held doc with no retirement condition** — the next reader inherits the caution without the reasoning.

⚠️ **Honest residual, stated rather than papered over:** an agent that acts and then dies before committing the receipt leaves the entry unmarked, and the next reader repeats the work. **That is the safe direction, and for a pointer-style entry repeating is usually cheap or idempotent — but it is not zero.**

## 5 · Reading and watching your own inbox

> ### 🔴 AT SPIN-UP, ASK JUSTIN TWO SEPARATE QUESTIONS — they are different acts with different costs.
>
> ***"Should I read my inbox?"*** **and** ***"Should I arm a watcher on it?"***

- **READING is cheap, and it closes the one gap nothing else covers.** **A watcher's baseline is the moment you armed it, so a message that landed BEFORE you existed is invisible to it permanently and silently** — and a handover lands in exactly that gap. **If he says yes, read it top to bottom before you arm anything.**
- **ARMING decides whose traffic wakes you, which is why it is his call and not yours.** ⚠️ **Two instances of one lineage can share an inbox**, so an unasked-for watcher wakes you on a peer's mail all day.

🔴 **ABSENCE OF A NOTIFICATION IS NOT EVIDENCE OF ABSENCE.** **Any mechanism whose failure mode is *silence* needs a second mechanism whose output is *unconditional*.** **For a watcher, that second mechanism is the spin-up read** — unconditional, and it runs at the one moment a watcher structurally cannot see.

**Watcher mechanics** — the full shape, the cost model and the arming traps live in `ac.md` (Autonomous Work Protocol). The two that bite here:

- ⚠️ **Watch `git log --all -1 -- <your inbox>` as well as the file's mtime** — the other side commits from a different worktree, which never moves your `HEAD`.
- ✅ **Do NOT try to detect the author.** Emit `CHANGED — read the file` and go read it. **In a per-recipient inbox this is nearly moot** — everything in your inbox is for you — but the rule stands: **an instrument that guesses can lie to you, and a redundant read costs seconds while a suppressed message is unrecoverable.**
- 🔴 **`TaskStop` the watcher when you set down.** An orphaned watcher fires forever and bills a full turn every time.

⚠️ **Do NOT have your heartbeat re-check your inbox every tick.** **A watcher firing wakes you by itself**, so a heartbeat that re-prints inbox state pays full turns to re-answer a question the watcher answers for free. **The heartbeat's only job is *"are you still moving?"*** *(See `ac.md`; this was corrected on 2026-08-20 after a heartbeat printing shared-doc state cost three full turns per tick to learn nothing.)*

## 6 · 🔴 RENAMING — the agent and the file are different questions

> **The rule, and the nuance that makes it usable:** **a rename of the AGENT does not automatically re-point the FILE — but the file CAN be renamed when nothing is watching it.**

**Why the file's path is protected at all: renaming it breaks every watcher armed on it, and it breaks them SILENTLY.** The watcher stays alive, keeps polling a path that no longer changes, and **reports nothing forever.** ⚠️ **A watch that dies with its session is bad; a watch pointed at a renamed file is worse, because its silence is indistinguishable from a quiet channel.**

🔑 **But a watcher is SESSION-BOUND — a `Monitor` dies with the session that armed it.** *(Established 2026-08-20.)* ⇒ **A rename can only break watchers that are LIVE AT THE MOMENT OF THE RENAME.**

> ### ⇒ THE RULE: **never rename a mailbox while a watcher is armed on it.** Not *"never rename a mailbox."*
>
> **The window is real and it recurs** — between sessions, nothing is watching.

**When you do rename one, all five steps, in order:**

1. **Confirm with Justin that no live session holds a watcher on it.** 🔴 **Only he can see that** — it is a claim about session state, and an agent cannot check it.
2. **`git mv` the file**, messages intact. **Update the inbox's own header; do not edit anyone's entries.**
3. **Post the mapping into every peer's inbox** — write into *their* file, per rule 1 — so they re-point rather than discovering it by silence.
4. **Repoint live references only.** ⚠️ **Changelogs, `docs/historical/**`, `docs/sessions/historical/**` and superseded handoffs are FROZEN RECORDS — a corrected path inside one is a falsification.**
5. **Record old → new in the roster below, and never delete the mapping.**

📌 **Worked example — `inbox-ac0.md` → `inbox-acp.md`, 2026-08-21.** The `AC0` → `ACP` rename happened on 2026-08-19 and **the file deliberately kept its old path at the time, because watchers were armed on it.** It moved two days later, at Justin's direction, **in a window when nothing was watching** — which is the distinction this section exists to make.

## 7 · Commit immediately after writing

**Write, then commit, then walk away.** 🔴 **Mailbox commits are PRE-AUTHORIZED — they do not wait for Justin's go-ahead.** All AC instances run on the same machine, so a **committed** message is visible to the other side with no push; push is only for GitHub and cross-machine durability.

⚠️ **An uncommitted message is recoverable only from your own working tree**, and a stray `git reset` or branch switch erases it.
