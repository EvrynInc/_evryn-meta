## §1 — The question, and why Justin thinks it matters

`evryn-team-runtime` composes each agent **fresh, every wake**: identity + memory + the thread transcript + ambient context are assembled into a new prompt, the agent acts, the wake ends.

**Justin's observation, which is correct at source:** *"You've got the whole thread… but that's kind of it. You don't have access to your thinking during that turn, or any of the other deep layers — just the superficial layer."*

**Verified:** `src/wakes/index.ts` is the only place a main's model is called, and it passes **no `resume`, no `sessionId`, no `continue`.** So what survives a wake is: identity, `MEMORY.md`, the thread transcript (including the agent's own past replies), digest blurbs, the private scratchpad, and any note deliberately written. **What does NOT survive: the agent's reasoning, its tool calls and their results, anything it loaded mid-wake, and every intermediate turn** — up to 40 of them.

**⇒ The concrete cost, and it is real:** an agent that spends a wake investigating something and concludes *"nothing here yet, I'll watch it"* has, by the next wake, **no memory of having investigated at all. It cannot know what it already ruled out.**

Justin's word for it was *"a disaster,"* and **he is reconsidering the decision to avoid SDK sessions** — *"if we keep threads under control, it's not a terrible idea. And if we give the agents a standing order to announce if they compact, that even keeps that danger from being a problem."*

**This would reverse part of ADR-052 §3.2. It is the most important open question in this lane.**

## §2 — What has already been established, so you do not redo it

**ACfm's prior analysis** (`_evryn-meta/docs/working/2026.08.11-acfm-acf-runtime-questions.md`, Q1) reached these, and you should **treat them as leads to verify, not settled**:

- **The original objection was that SDK compaction summarises history opaquely and un-steerably** — a summariser whose output we do not control. Fresh composition keeps every byte enumerable, logged in the wake manifest, and diffable.
- **Justin's counter is genuinely strong:** compaction only fires when a context grows large, and Step 4's membership model plus Step 5's 7-day age-out exist precisely to keep rooms small — so under tight membership **most threads should never reach compaction at all.**
- **⚠️ But the counter-counter has not been reckoned with:** an announcement tells you compaction *happened*; **it cannot tell you what was lost** — that is the nature of the failure. And **a session's contents are not enumerable in the wake manifest**, which is one of this runtime's genuinely unusual strengths (ARCHITECTURE cardinal invariant 2).
- **The cheaper alternative that targets the actual pain:** the per-(agent, thread) **scratchpad already exists and already composes back into that room's wakes.** Nothing today instructs an agent to use it as a working record. *"Before you finish, put in your scratchpad what you looked at and what you ruled out"* recovers most of the lost value at **zero architectural cost**, and it is steerable and inspectable.

**🔴 The open UNKNOWN that gates everything:** *does an SDK session survive a process restart?* The whole runtime rests on ARCHITECTURE cardinal invariant 4 — **everything durable is in Postgres; crash → restart → re-arm losslessly.** **A session living in SDK-side state would be the first thing in this system that is not.**
