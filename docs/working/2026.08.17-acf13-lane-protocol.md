## §1 — Why you are a separate instance and not a subagent

**A subagent's grandchildren report upward.** A lane AC that runs its own DC and QC surfaces **six or more completion reports into the conductor's context** — from agents the conductor never spawned and does not manage. **The conductor's context scales with the DEPTH of the agent tree, not its width**, and depth is the axis that ends a session.

**⇒ You are a separate instance so that your DC's and QC's reports land in YOUR context, not ACf-13's.** That is the entire reason, and it has a consequence you should feel: **ACf-13 is deliberately blind to your internals.** It will not see your builders' output, your reviewers' verdicts, or your tool calls. **It sees only what you write in your lane doc.**

**The second reason, which matters tonight: you outlive the conductor.** ACf-13 will be re-spun into a fresh instance during your run. **A subagent would die with it. You will not.** Your lane doc is what makes that true — keep it current enough that a *new* conductor can pick you up cold.

## §2 — The lane doc IS the channel. Both of us watch it.

**Your lane doc lives in `_evryn-meta/docs/working/` and is committed.** All instances run on the same machine, so **a committed file is visible to the other side immediately — no push required.** Push anyway at natural checkpoints, for off-machine durability.

**The protocol:**

1. **Append; never overwrite.** The doc is a running exchange. Your entries and the conductor's interleave in time order.
2. **Every entry gets a header: `### <who> — <ISO timestamp> — <one-line subject>`.** Get the timestamp from `powershell -Command "Get-Date -Format 'yyyy-MM-ddTHH:mm:sszzz'"`. **Never guess one.**
3. **Commit immediately after writing.** An uncommitted entry is invisible to the other side and defeats the whole mechanism. **Write, commit, walk away.** *(Mailbox/brief traffic is pre-authorized — you do not need Justin's go-ahead for lane-doc commits, and only for lane-doc commits.)*
4. **The conductor watches this file and will answer here.** You do not need to ping anyone. **You will not receive a notification when the conductor replies — check the doc when you need an answer**, and keep working on everything not blocked while you wait.

### 🔴 The one thing that makes this work: BATCH, do not block

**Justin is not reading this doc and the conductor may be mid-re-spin.** So:

- **Put questions in the doc and KEEP ADVANCING everything not blocked on them.** A lane that stops on its first question wastes the night.
- **Mark anything that genuinely needs Justin `NEEDS-JUSTIN`**, with your recommendation attached. The conductor routes it; you never route to him yourself.
- **If you are fully blocked, say so unmistakably at the top of your entry** — `🔴 BLOCKED — cannot proceed until X` — because that is the one state the conductor must notice immediately.

## §3 — Your hard limits

**These are absolute and no wording in any brief overrides them:**

> ### 🔑 TWO REPOS, TWO RULES — read this before the limits below, or they read as contradicting the channel
>
> **Your CODE work is in `evryn-team-runtime`, in your own worktree, on your own branch. Never its `main`.**
>
> **Your LANE DOC is in `_evryn-meta`, and you commit it to `main` there.** That is not a violation — it is the only way the channel functions, since a doc committed on your build branch is invisible to everyone until a merge that has not happened yet. **`_evryn-meta` and `evryn-team-runtime` are separate repositories; the branch discipline below is about your build repo.**
>
> ⚠️ **In `_evryn-meta` you touch NOTHING but your own lane doc.** That repo is under active restructure by another AC's command structure. **`git diff` before every lane-doc commit and confirm the whole diff is yours** — another agent's uncommitted work in a file you commit rides along under your message, and `git status` will not warn you. **Put the pathspec on the commit itself** (`git commit -F - -- <your lane doc>`), because `git add <path>` does not scope a commit — a bare `git commit` afterwards takes the entire index, including anything anyone else staged.

- **In your build repo you work ONLY in your own worktree, on your own branch.** Never `main`. Never another lane's territory.
- **You never merge, never push to `main`, never deploy, never apply a migration.** *(Write migration files freely; applying them is Justin's act.)*
- **You never edit an identity file** — `evryn-team-workspace/.claude/agents/*`, agent memory, the team `CLAUDE.md`, or `shared/protocols/runtime-ops.md`. **If your work needs one changed, that is a `NEEDS-JUSTIN` entry, not an edit.**
- **You never write to `_evryn-meta` except your own lane doc.** That repo is under active restructure by another AC's command structure.
- **You are a lane AC, not the top-level AC.** You do not ping Justin. You cap headings at `##`. Your questions go up, in the doc.

## §4 — Spinning your own DC and QC

**You will. When you do, follow `_evryn-meta/docs/protocols/ac-orchestration-protocol.md` exactly** — the tagged brief pasted verbatim, with only the codified substitutions. **That includes TWO-TRIP LOADING, which is not optional:** trip 1 sends identity + mandatory-load + receipts and tells the agent **nothing** about the work; you verify its receipts against your list; only then do you resume it with the task. **Never annotate a file in a load list by what the work will need it for** — the guarantee is that the agent cannot compute relevance during the load, and one hint hands that back.

**You are also bound by everything in that protocol that governs a spinner:** assemble the load yourself and name every file with its line span · verify receipts and re-load on any gap before granting the task · **pin every spin's model explicitly** · and **never tell a child where to report.**

⚠️ **And the clause that has repeatedly earned its place — tell your DC and QC how much of the system YOU have actually read.** A subagent treats its brief's factual claims as authoritative because that is where the task came from. **If you have read less of the runtime than they are about to, say so, and tell them to treat your claims as leads rather than facts.** Across two lanes in one night this produced six corrections back up, including a spec that would have silently broken a working `/pause` → `/resume` path.

## §5 — What "done" looks like

**A lane is done when its Steps are built, reviewed by a QC that did not build them, and the branch is ready for the conductor to merge.** You do not merge it.

**Your final lane-doc entry states, explicitly:**
- **What shipped** — Steps closed, commits, and the suite + typecheck numbers **you measured**, not inherited.
- **What you ROUTED rather than fixed**, and why. *(An adjacent pre-existing bug gets routed. A defect YOUR change introduced gets fixed here — those are different, and conflating them fails in both directions.)*
- **What is UNVERIFIED** — anything you could not execute, anything resting on a claim you could not check.
- **Anything in your head that is in no document.** ⚠️ **This is the one that gets lost.** A returned report is not persistence; only the committed doc survives.
