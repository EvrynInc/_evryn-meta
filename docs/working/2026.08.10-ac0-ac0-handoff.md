### 6.3 🔑 THE ATLAS SCOUT TEAM — AND ITS WRITE-SAFETY PROTOCOL

**Justin's concern, and he was right to raise it:** *"agents writing straight to the atlas — that sounds risky… I can see agents mid-flight making errors — how do we keep this from getting fucked up?"*

**The naive version IS dangerous.** Several agents writing one file concurrently means conflicts, clobbering, and a half-written artifact that reads as authoritative. **The protocol below removes the danger structurally rather than instructing against it.**

1. **ONE FILE PER SUBSYSTEM, ONE AUTHOR PER FILE.** The Atlas is a **directory** (`docs/atlas/`), not a monolith — `ingest.md`, `triage.md`, `safety.md`, `approval.md`, `data.md`. **No two agents ever open the same file, so a write conflict is not merely unlikely — it is impossible.** This is also better for readers: load the section you need, which is the Atlas's entire purpose.
2. **Each scout works in its OWN worktree on its OWN branch.** Nothing reaches `main` mid-flight. A confused agent damages only its own branch.
3. **EVERY CLAIM CITES `file:line`.** This is the load-bearing rule. **A mid-flight error that cites a line is FINDABLE; an uncited assertion is baked in forever.** It is also what makes the Atlas re-verifiable later — a pass can check that every cited line still says what the Atlas claims.
4. **The INDEX is written LAST, by one agent, after the sections land.** Never concurrently.
5. **Each section merges only after its author re-validates its claims against the code as it stands then, plus a reviewer pass.** Same gate as code. **Section by section — the Atlas never lands in one shot.**
6. **Every scout loads the dependency map** (`evryn-backend/docs/dependency-map.md`, machine-generated, on `main` as of `2a1bdab`). **That is the seam layer, and it is why this is possible now and was not yesterday** — no scout is blind to how its slice connects, because the connections are a document.
7. **Keep the scouts alive as a bench.** Resumable all session. A cross-seam question gets asked of **both** sides and reconciled — two specialists disagreeing is a better seam check than one generalist remembering. ⚠️ **Resume dies at session end, so anything durable must be written down.**

**The blast-radius fact worth holding: they are writing DOCS, not code.** A wrong section is caught by review and never touches the runtime. **But do not relax on that** — our own standing rule is that a *stale or wrong* Atlas is worse than no Atlas, because an agent trusting a bad map reasons confidently from a system that does not exist. **Rule 3 is what makes a wrong claim cheap to find.**
