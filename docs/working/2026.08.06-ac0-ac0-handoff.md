## §1 — WHAT CHANGED TODAY. THIS IS THE PART THAT MAKES YOU DIFFERENT FROM YESTERDAY'S AC0.

### 1.1 🔴 TWO-TRIP LOADING — the orchestration protocol was rewritten. Do not brief from memory.

**Justin's design.** Every subagent spin is now **two trips**:

- **Trip 1 — the load.** Send `<identity>` + `<mandatory_load>` + `<receipts>`. **Say NOTHING about the work** — not the task, not the goal, not why any file matters. Its entire output is receipts.
- **You verify** the receipts against your list. Gap → resume it, name the gap, it re-loads.
- **Trip 2 — the work.** Only then, resume with `<task>` (+ `<questions_first>`, `<isolation>`).

**Why it works:** every subagent that skipped a briefed file described the same mechanism — *"I triage the load list against the task as I read it… I never registered a moment of choosing to drop it."* **That triage needs a task to triage against.** Remove the task from the load trip and the failure is **structurally unavailable**, not merely discouraged.

**What got deleted:** the pleading, the all-caps, the threats, the *"we already spun you once and you failed"* callout, the `STOP SKIMPING` rant, and the whole `<pre_task>` block. **They existed to talk an agent out of a triage it can no longer perform.**

🔴 **THE COST, AND YOU MUST HOLD IT:** the subagent can no longer drop a file — **but it can no longer notice one YOU left out either**, for the same reason. **Your list is now the entire load with nothing behind it.** Under the old model a good subagent might have flagged your gap; now nobody will.

**The mitigation, added at Justin's direction:** `<task>` (trip 2 only) now carries **explicit permission to load MORE** — plus an obligation to **report anything loaded beyond the list, with one line on why.** That report is your only signal that your list had a gap. **Read it every time; it is how your next brief gets better.**
