## §1 — 🔴 THE AUDIT IS NOT A NICE-TO-HAVE. HERE ARE THREE PROOFS.

The most important thing in this brief is this section, so read it before §2.

In a **single session** on 2026-08-04, AC0-28 loaded the full runtime and found **three separate cases where the system's own documentation and code comments assert something that is not true.** These weren't hunted for. They surfaced incidentally.

**Exhibit A — the 6-month TTL does not exist.**
`ARCHITECTURE.md` documents a 6-month TTL on `emailmgr_items` (forwarded work items). It's referenced across the docs as an established property. **There is no TTL.** Not in `src/` — no cleanup job, no scheduled delete, nothing. Not in the database — no `pg_cron` job (the `pg_cron` references in the SQL dumps are Supabase's stock boilerplate, not ours). Verified both layers.

**And it's worse than absence: `src/` reasons *from* it.** Two comments in the shipped runtime treat it as real — one in `src/triage/classify.ts` explaining that a reference "TTL'd out with the item," another in `src/triage/preload.ts` reasoning about "a beyond-TTL junk re-sender." **We have been designing against a mechanism that was never built.** Justin's reaction, verbatim: *"because that TTL is talked about everywhere, so if it's not there, who the fuck knows what else is missing."* That question is your assignment.

**Exhibit B — "the sole Anthropic spend door" is false.**
Three places in the runtime (`src/triage/classify.ts`, `src/safety/breaker.ts`, `src/db/llm-usage.ts`) state that `runEvrynQuery` is the single function every AI call passes through — which is the premise the cost circuit-breaker's attempt-counting rests on. It isn't: `runHaikuScreen` → `defaultRunQuery` (`src/triage/haiku-screen.ts`) calls the SDK's `query()` **directly**, so Haiku spend never reaches `recordAttemptAndCheck` and cannot trip the breaker. *(No money is actually at risk — a separate breaker-aware early-return covers it. The defect is that the comment is load-bearing and wrong.)*

**Exhibit C — `docs/schema-reference.md` misdescribes the live database.**
Three tables that exist in production are absent from it; one dropped table is still documented as live; one column is marked "not yet built" that shipped months ago. It carries a DO-NOT-TRUST banner as a stopgap.

**The pattern:** these are three instances of one disease — *we believe things about this system that aren't true, and we build on those beliefs.* Two of them (A and B) are **inside the shipped code, in comments a future builder will reason from.** That's the class of thing that doesn't announce itself; it just makes someone confidently wrong six months later.

**So the audit's question is not "are the docs tidy?" It is: for every claim this system makes about itself, is it true?** I expect that pass to be worth more than the extraction.

## §2 — YOUR FOUR DELIVERABLES

### 1. The dependency map — cheap and regenerable

A machine-derived map of what imports what across `src/`. **The word "regenerable" is the requirement:** a hand-drawn map is stale the week after it's drawn, and we'd be adding a fourth exhibit to §1. Ship it as a script that regenerates the map, plus the current output. Static analysis of the import graph — nothing clever.

**This is first because it makes everything after it cheaper**, including your own extraction (it tells you exactly who depends on `classify.ts` and on what).

### 2. The Code Atlas

The artifact that lets a future AC load *less* and still be competent. Roughly: for each module — what it's for, what it owns, what depends on it, what it depends on, and the non-obvious invariants a reader must not break.

**Design it against a hard test:** *could a fresh AC read the Atlas plus 3–4 named files and do a scoped piece of work correctly?* If the honest answer is no, it isn't done. Concretely — could AC5 (the ingest lane, briefed separately) have gotten their file list from the Atlas instead of from me hand-deriving it after a full read?

**⚠️ The Atlas must never become a substitute for reading the runtime on work that needs it.** This exact failure has burned us: docs were reconciled, people concluded they could skip `src/`, and it cost us. Write the Atlas to make a *targeted* load possible, and say so in the Atlas itself. The Atlas is an index, not a replacement.

### 3. 🔴 The verification pass — the claims audit

**Justin's explicit ask** (2026-08-04): *"one pass to just look at the things that we think are present and actually verify that they are."*

Walk the claims this system makes about itself — in `ARCHITECTURE.md`, the BUILD docs, and **especially in code comments** — and check each against reality. Exhibits A–C in §1 are your seed set and your calibration: they show both the *kind* of claim that rots and how badly.

Where to look hardest, based on where the three known cases live:
- **Claims about mechanisms that "exist"** — TTLs, cleanup jobs, scheduled processes, retention. *(A whole class: anything asserting a background process nobody watches. The TTL was exactly this.)*
- **Claims about structural invariants** — "the sole X", "the only path to Y", "nothing else writes Z". These are the dangerous ones because safety properties get built on them. Exhibit B is one; **check every other "sole/only/never" claim in the runtime.**
- **Claims about the database** — every table, column, constraint, and RPC the docs describe vs. what's actually in prod.
- **Claims about what's deployed vs. merged vs. built.** `built ≠ merged ≠ deployed` and the docs blur it.

**Output:** a findings list, each with *the claim · where it's asserted · what's actually true · severity · who owns the fix.* **Do not fix them as you go** — some will be doc edits (cheap), some will be real builds (the TTL is), and some will be things Justin needs to decide. Route them; don't absorb them. **Flag anything genuinely dangerous to AC0 immediately rather than waiting for the final list.**

**A known one to fold in:** the TTL should become **1 year, not 6 months** (Justin's call, 2026-08-04 — a gatekeeper's inbound stays relevant well past six months, and we'd want to re-run it against changed criteria). Since nothing exists to change, that's currently a doc correction plus a future build. **Do not build the TTL in this lane** — a scheduled delete against production data deserves its own trip.

### 4. 🔴 Extract `classify.ts` — and stabilize it before Gate-B

**Justin's ruling, 2026-08-04:** *"extract now — then make fucking sure it's stable before Gate B — that way Gate B is actually testing the real runtime."*

**That reasoning is the constraint.** Gate-B (targeted **Friday 2026-08-07**) is the mandatory pre-gatekeeper full live-fire. Its whole value is that it exercises the real system. Extract *after* it and the verdict describes a runtime that no longer exists; extract *before* and Gate-B validates what we actually ship. So: **extract early in the week, stabilize hard, and Gate-B runs against the extracted tree.**

**My read of the seams — verify it, don't take it.** `classify.ts` currently fuses at least four distinct concerns:
- **Identity composition** — `loadCommonPrefix` / `loadOperatorSuffix` / `loadUserSuffix` / `composeSystemPrompt` / `buildPersonContext` / `formatConversationHistory` / `wrapHistoryReferenceOnly`.
- **The MCP tool surface** — `buildEvrynMcpServer` and all the tool definitions (by far the bulk).
- **The query entry point** — `runEvrynQuery`, its M1 breaker gates, the abort/error classification, usage capture.
- **Pure helpers + validators** — `buildPassStamp`, `gatekeeperFirstName`, `validateUserIsValidTarget`, the note-turn guards, `registerGatekeeperAddress`.

You own the actual decomposition. Use your own dependency map (deliverable 1) to drive it.

**⚠️ TWO HAZARDS — both non-obvious, both expensive if missed:**

1. **🔴 This is a PURE refactor. Behavior must be byte-identical.** No fixes, no cleanups, no "while I'm in here." Every behavior change you're tempted to make becomes a Gate-B variable. Write them down and route them instead.

2. **🔴 `loadCommonPrefix()` must produce a BYTE-IDENTICAL string after extraction — this is a money property, not a style one.** Anthropic's prompt cache is prefix-based: a byte-identical system prompt across calls costs ~10% of a fresh one. That cached prefix is the difference, in Emma's runway numbers, between roughly six weeks and roughly two. `loadCommonPrefix` builds its string by globbing identity directories, sorting alphabetically, and joining with a specific separator — **change the ordering, the separator, the filter, or the join by one character and the cache silently misses and costs jump.** Nothing will fail. Nothing will alert. The bill just goes up. **Assert byte-identity explicitly in a test** — that test is the single most valuable artifact of this deliverable.
