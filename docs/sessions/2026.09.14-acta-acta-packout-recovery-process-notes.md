## 2 · Hazards this recovery actually hit — each is cheap to prevent

- 🔴 **A compacted instance misattributes its predecessor's last actions to itself.** ACT-A told Justin it committed the handoff *after* compacting; the commit predates the compaction boundary (P2-11). ⇒ **Never trust a compacted instance's account of what it did around the compaction. Compare commit timestamps against the transcript's `compact_boundary` record.**
- 🔴 **A home-made receipts check produced a false negative about a canary** (P2-12). It matched `FULL FILE LOADED` anywhere in a window — so a file whose header *quotes* the canary shows it on window one — and it ignored non-`Read` confirmations such as `tail -2`. ⇒ **Judge only a file's last window, and look for other confirmations before declaring a canary unseen.**
- **Self-estimated context runs 2–3× off** (P2-04: "150–200K left" written minutes before Justin measured 67K). **Justin can read a session's context directly — ask him at clean points instead of trusting the count.**
- **Subagents cannot converse with each other.** `SendMessage`'s own documentation: a subagent's sends go out under its parent session's address, and replies return to the parent. ⇒ **Instances that must talk to each other are standalone sessions.**
- **A pathspec commit of a shared log carries every other party's UNCOMMITTED append.** It happened once here (`428cab3` carried ACT-A's sign-off). ⇒ **Before committing a shared log, confirm the other parties' latest entries are already in `HEAD`; if not, wait.**
- **`Monitor` differs per session:** ACT-A's accepted `persistent: true`; P1's, P2's and P3's had no such field and a 30-minute cap. ⇒ **`ac.md`'s "HOW TO ARM ONE" recipe does not work everywhere; re-arm on expiry where it doesn't.** *(Routed for Justin; not edited.)*
- **Forecasting the next report's number breaks every time an interim report goes out** — it moved #4 → #5 → #6 → #7 → #8 here. ⇒ **Never promise a future report's number; assign it when the report is sent.**
- **Justin's heartbeat correction (raw L1190):** with instances in flight, keep the heartbeat armed even while blocked on him — *"if one or the other of them freezes and you are asleep the whole time, you'll never see it."*

### Added 2026-09-16, after round 1 lost two of its three instances

- 🔴 **ONE JOB PER HEAD.** Both instances that compacted carried a second heavy job on top of the full ~400K load:
  - **P1:** a transcript re-sweep after its audit;
  - **P3:** two transcript parts, both handoffs, and about forty small edits.

  **P2, which built the handoff and was stood down near its limit, never compacted.** ⇒ **Split by job, not by transcript length alone. A closer who fixes the document should not also be a reader of the transcript.**
- 🔴 **A compacted instance committed work AFTER compacting** (P3, `4f667fc`). Its edits may be sound, but nobody can tell, so the next round had to spend a check on them. ⇒ **Every compaction block says: commit ONLY the one stop line, and name any uncommitted work instead of committing it.**
- **An Admiral cannot rescue a Captain's unposted draft.** Copying another session's scratchpad into the repo was refused by the permission classifier — and rightly not routed around. ⇒ **Captains post ledgers and findings into the log, in committed batches, as they go.** *"Draft in the scratchpad until done"* is only safe for text that can be re-derived.
- **`Monitor` behaviour changed within ONE conversation across a session restart:** `persistent: true` was honoured on 2026-09-14, and a 30-minute cap applied on 2026-09-16. ⇒ **Check the arm result every time; do not carry forward what worked before a restart.**

### Added 2026-09-16, after round 2 finished with all three instances standing

- ✅ **WHAT WORKED: three heads with one job each, one writer on the handoff, and findings posted in committed batches as they went.**
  - **Pa and Pb swept the transcript and posted findings only; Pc read no transcript, applied every fix, and kept the ledger.**
  - **Pc posted a `LEDGER DRAFT` early and staged its `PACKOUT COMPLETE` body before its final fixes.** ⇒ **No compaction could have lost either.**
- 🔴 **A BLOCKED INSTANCE'S WATCHER BURNS ITS CONTEXT.** Every entry another party posts wakes it and adds that entry to its context.
  - ⇒ **Once a Captain is blocked and near its budget, have it stop BOTH instruments, and wake it with a `SendMessage` when its input lands.** *(R10: all three did this at ~800–880K and finished.)*
- **Justin's context readings are tokens USED, and compaction comes at 1M.**
  - **The ~400K load estimate held;** his readings included real work on top of it.
  - **The dangerous move near the edge is one big read.** Every `Read` window caps at ~25K, so a **900K relay line** leaves room for any single step.
- 🔴 **THE PATHSPEC SWEEP HAPPENED AGAIN (`4e957c4`), despite the rule.** A Captain's commit chained straight after its `git diff` and carried the Admiral's uncommitted entry.
  - ⇒ **The fix that held: an append script that REFUSES to write while the log has any uncommitted change**, and a diff run as its own step before committing.
- **An enumeration beats a search for "what did Justin say after line N?"** Brief §7.4's line list came from a search and missed two of his messages (L1856, L1970); **walking every record by type found them.**
- ⚠️ **On Windows, Node's `execSync` runs through `cmd.exe`, where `^` is an escape character** — so `git cat-file -e <sha>^{commit}` failed for every SHA, and **reported all 43 cited commits missing.**
  - **Known-good controls exposed it.** ⇒ **Use `execFileSync` with no shell, and always include a control.**
