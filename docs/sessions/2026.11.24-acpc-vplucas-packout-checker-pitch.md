## 1 · The shape

- **The outgoing seat checks live state once, then reads its own handoff and start line** in fresh-eyes passes, up to five, until one comes back clean. The passes are reading only.
- **It then tells the seat one layer up "ready for review."** That seat starts a **Packout Checker**: a fresh, light worker, by command, from a fixed start line. The Checker reads the outgoing seat's transcript by script, builds its own list of what the seat did, and walks every packout and lock step against it. It hands gaps back to the outgoing seat until they're fixed, posts one verdict to the Log, and ends. **It judges steps, never content, and launches no one.**
- **The incoming seat's first act, once its load is verified (by whoever launched it, as now; the Checker doesn't verify loads), is its own live-state pass,** covering everything after the handoff's last save. *(Justin, B14: "outgoing does its own passes; incoming does the live-state check ... makes the most sense, no?")*
- **The packout starts earlier:** tail out at ~700K, start the packout at ~750K, be done by ~950K (his B13 (a)).
- **What retires:** the old Checker seat (a full Lucas at about 500K of load, lasting about five checks) and the separately loaded fresh reader that today's packout step 5 spins. **The guard script (`packout.mjs`) is not part of this.** The Checker runs without it until task T-153 returns to it (his B15).

## 3 · The packout protocol (`docs/protocols/packout-protocol.md`): exact words

### 3.1 · Step 5: replace the whole step

**Replace** everything from `### 5 · Do one more fresh-eyes pass of the OUTGOING handoff alone` down to (not including) `### 6 · Retire the old brief`. That span holds the spin-a-fresh-instance block, the "Hunting errors…" test, the original-author block and the three-route block. **Replace it with:**

> ### 5 · Run your own fresh-eyes passes over the OUTGOING handoff and its start line, until one comes back clean (at most five)
>
> *(Justin, 2026-11-24: "[the outgoing instance is] the most-contextualized *anyone could be* to do that pass ... with *no* extra load ... keep doing fresh-eyes reads until you aren't finding mistakes anymore." Capped at five.)*
>
> **First, once, before your first pass: check the live state, so your handoff is true when you finish it.** Use the cheap forms; none of these needs a whole-file read:
> - every seat the handoff names: `node _evryn-meta/scripts/context-size.mjs --session <id>`;
> - every session it names: `claude agents --all --json`, filtered to name and state (`| node -e` keeping those two fields);
> - every repo you wrote: `git status --porcelain` and `git log origin/<branch>..<branch> --oneline`, against your `## Uncommitted` list;
> - the Log since your handoff's first commit: `git -C <repo> diff <that commit> -- <the Log>`, the new lines only;
> - every launch or commit your start line asks of your successor, against its limits.
>
> Fix what differs in your handoff or start line, and record the check in your gate. Your passes then read the fix, so it needs no separate re-read.
>
> **Each pass:** Read the handoff and its start line whole, with the Read tool, after your last edit to either, as a stranger would read them. **The test is one question: *could a brand-new instance, holding none of your current context, understand this fully and act on it?*** Hunt errors, omissions, staleness and anything unclear. Fix what you find in your own handoff, then start the next pass.
>
> **Stop when a pass finds nothing to change.** Its Reads, with no edit after them, are the proof. **Stop at five regardless:** if the fifth still found something, say so in your gate, and the Packout Checker takes it to your manager. **Record each pass in your gate:** its number, the Reads' line spans, and what it found ("nothing" for the last).
>
> **These are reading passes.** You check live state once, before them, and your successor checks it again on arrival. **Why you do your own passes instead of having a separate reader do them:** you're the most-contextualized reader there is, at no extra load. The weakness is that a gap can fill itself from your session memory and read as complete. **Reading the file and not your recollection, and repeating until a pass is clean, is what works against it.**

**⚠️ One placement changed from B14 (a), on Justin's context worry. He asked of the gate version, "what are we trying to accomplish? Could diffing work?"**
- **What the check is for:** the handoff should be true when written; everything after its last save is the successor's check.
- **Why it moved:** at the gate, any fix the check caused needed a whole re-read of the changed file, which is the context cost he was worried about. **Run once before the passes, the passes read the fix for free.**
- **Diffing:** the Log, the one possibly big input, is read as a `git diff` of its new lines only. The session list is filtered to two fields.
