## §1 — Why you are a separate instance and not a subagent

**A subagent's grandchildren report upward.** A lane AC that runs its own DC and QC surfaces **six or more completion reports into the conductor's context** — from agents the conductor never spawned and does not manage. **The conductor's context scales with the DEPTH of the agent tree, not its width**, and depth is the axis that ends a session.

**⇒ You are a separate instance so that your DC's and QC's reports land in YOUR context, not ACf-13's.** That is the entire reason, and it has a consequence you should feel: **ACf-13 is deliberately blind to your internals.** It will not see your builders' output, your reviewers' verdicts, or your tool calls. **It sees only what you write in your lane doc.**

**The second reason, which matters tonight: you outlive the conductor.** ACf-13 will be re-spun into a fresh instance during your run. **A subagent would die with it. You will not.** Your lane doc is what makes that true — keep it current enough that a *new* conductor can pick you up cold.

## §2 — The lane doc IS the channel. Both of us watch it.

**Your lane doc lives in `_evryn-meta/docs/working/` and is committed.** All instances run on the same machine, so **a committed file is visible to the other side immediately — no push required.** Push anyway at natural checkpoints, for off-machine durability.

**The protocol:**

1. **Append; never overwrite.** The doc is a running exchange. Your entries and the conductor's interleave in time order.
2. **Every entry gets a header: `### <who> — <ISO timestamp> — <one-line subject>`.** Get the timestamp from `powershell -Command "Get-Date -Format 'yyyy-MM-ddTHH:mm:sszzz'"`. **Never guess one.**
3. **Commit immediately after writing.** An uncommitted entry is invisible to the other side and defeats the whole mechanism. **Write, commit, walk away.** *(Mailbox/brief traffic is pre-authorized — you do not need Justin's go-ahead for lane-doc commits, and only for lane-doc commits.)*
4. **The conductor watches this file and will answer here.** You do not need to ping anyone. **You will not receive a notification when the conductor replies — check the doc when you need an answer**, and keep working on everything not blocked while you wait.

### 🔴 ARM YOUR OWN WATCHER, AND YOUR OWN CANARY. Both, before you start work.

**You will NOT be notified when the conductor replies in your doc. Watch it yourself.** `Monitor` streams each stdout line into your chat as a notification, and a notification wakes you — so a script that prints a line when your doc grows is a working inbox:

```
Monitor({ description: "<your name> inbox", persistent: true, timeout_ms: 3600000,
  command: 'D="$HOME/Evryn/Code/_evryn-meta/docs/working"; cd "$D" || { echo "🔴 WATCHER DID NOT START: $D does not exist - it is watching NOTHING."; exit 1; }; f=<YOUR LANE DOC>; [ -f "$f" ] || { echo "🔴 WATCHER DID NOT START: lane doc $f not found in $D."; exit 1; }; n=$(wc -l < "$f"); while true; do sleep 45; m=$(wc -l < "$f"); if [ "$m" != "$n" ]; then echo "CONDUCTOR REPLIED in $f ($n -> $m lines) — read the bottom of the file."; n=$m; fi; done' })
```

**And arm a ~10-minute self-wake canary,** because the thing that ends an overnight lane is not usually a crash — **it is stopping without noticing.**

🔴 **AND MAKE THE CANARY RE-CHECK YOUR LANE DOC EVERY TICK — do not rely on the watcher alone.** *(Justin's design, 2026-08-17, after this exact failure: a reply landed in a shared doc, the conductor's watcher never fired, and it was found only because Justin asked directly.)*

**Why both, and why this is not redundancy:** **the watcher is EVENT-DRIVEN and can miss** — re-arming it resets its baseline, so anything that arrived during the gap is silently swallowed; a killed process misses everything. **The canary is a PERIODIC SWEEP that re-checks unconditionally, so a missed event is caught on the next tick.** ⇒ **Watcher for latency, canary for reliability. ABSENCE OF A NOTIFICATION IS NOT EVIDENCE OF ABSENCE.**

```
Monitor({ description: "<your name> self-wake — 10 min", persistent: true, timeout_ms: 3600000,
  command: 'f="$HOME/Evryn/Code/_evryn-meta/docs/working/<YOUR LANE DOC>"
[ -f "$f" ] || { echo "🔴 CANARY DID NOT START: $f not found - you have NO canary."; exit 1; }
while true; do sleep 600
  echo "CANARY $(date +%H:%M) — you may have stopped. Last heading in your lane doc: $(grep -E "^#{2,3} " "$f" | tail -1 | cut -c1-70)"
  echo "  If that is not YOUR last entry, the conductor has replied — go read it. Then CONTINUE where you left off."
done' })
```

⚠️ **`Monitor` is a DEFERRED tool — `ToolSearch` for it first** (`select:Monitor,TaskStop`). **Kill both with `TaskStop` when your lane is done.**

## §5 — What "done" looks like

**A lane is done when its Steps are built, reviewed by a QC that did not build them, and the branch is ready for the conductor to merge.** You do not merge it.

**Your final lane-doc entry states, explicitly:**
- **What shipped** — Steps closed, commits, and the suite + typecheck numbers **you measured**, not inherited.
- **What you ROUTED rather than fixed**, and why. *(An adjacent pre-existing bug gets routed. A defect YOUR change introduced gets fixed here — those are different, and conflating them fails in both directions.)*
- **What is UNVERIFIED** — anything you could not execute, anything resting on a claim you could not check.
- **Anything in your head that is in no document.** ⚠️ **This is the one that gets lost.** A returned report is not persistence; only the committed doc survives.
