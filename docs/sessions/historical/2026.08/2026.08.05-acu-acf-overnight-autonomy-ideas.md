## The core reframe (Justin's, and the thing worth holding onto)

**"In the day, Justin clears every blocker; overnight, everything just runs and runs."**

That is the design *goal* to aim the autonomy toward — not "agents work while he's away" (reactive), but a deliberate rhythm where the human bottleneck is **front-loaded into the day** so the night is unblocked. Justin's own caveat: *"maybe there's a functional limitation to this, but it's worth working toward at the very least."*

## The honest ceiling — name it, don't design around a wish

The reason this isn't trivially achievable: **the real bottleneck is mid-*build* questions, not startup ones.** An agent rarely knows at 4pm every decision it'll need by 2am. So the value of any "clear blockers by day, run at night" scheme is bounded by **how many of the night's questions can be anticipated and pre-cleared** vs. how many are genuinely blocking and only surface mid-flight. Design for that reality: the win is *reducing* the mid-flight blocks, not pretending they vanish.

## The pattern that attacks the ceiling — the ~4pm question-batch

The concrete mechanic Justin floated: **a conductor spins its sub-agents in the late afternoon; they work until they hit questions; the conductor answers everything it competently can and batches the genuine-Justin questions into one cleaned, recommend-first ballot; Justin clears the batch; the agents then run overnight against a much larger pre-cleared decision surface.**

**⇒ This is *exactly* the escalation ladder from `ac-orchestration-protocol.md` (the AC-under-AC work), applied to the team runtime.** The head agent's job — absorb what it can, surface only genuine-Justin calls, in batched recommend-first form, with the absorption itself visible — is a solved design over in the AC world. **Strong suggestion: reuse that thinking rather than reinventing it.** The team runtime's overnight question-surfacing and the escalation ladder are the same problem; a shared design is cheaper and more coherent.

## The self-wake mechanic (probably already covered — flagged for completeness)

Justin asked whether an agent could schedule its own re-invocation at a set time, to continue if it halted overnight. **ACU confirmed it's feasible** at the orchestration layer (a scheduled wake-up / cron re-invoke while the session is open, aligned to the rolling 5-hr usage-window boundaries so a halted agent resumes at each window). **But the team runtime is already a wake-based system** (ADR-052; `src/scheduler/`, `src/wakes/`) — so this is very likely *already built* on your side, and the note is only here so the idea isn't mistaken for missing. Evaluate against what exists; don't build a second wake path.
